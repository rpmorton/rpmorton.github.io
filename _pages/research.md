## Working Papers

### [Value Creation and Value Capture in Indian Garment Sector Bargaining](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SpxGm7bKFBeSrq-szBUWVIQKxTBr8SeM/view?usp=drive_link)

Low prices paid by large low-and-middle income country exporters to their small suppliers raise concerns about unequal distribution of gains from exporting. When insurance and credit markets are missing, buyers adopt contracts and organizational strategies that generate two sources of buyer power with different distributional implications. Contracts create additional surplus beyond the transfer of the input itself by providing insurance or trade credit; low prices then reflect the buyer's share of incremental surplus from providing insurance or trade credit. Partial vertical integration enables surplus capture through threats of replacement with in-house production. I estimate a structural bargaining model using proprietary data on fabric purchases by a large Indian garment exporter. Results show buyer power over surplus creation, not capture, explains low prices; the buyer retains ~60% of surplus created.
